🎚️ Weight what matters to you

six dimensions, in your browser only

The study refuses to collapse everything into one number. These sliders don't change that; they build a personal score on top, live, shown as the your score column in the table, as numbered 🟠①–⑤ picks on the 🎯 frontier chart above, and as the ranking in 🏆 venues below.

How to read a slider: ignore the abstract number and read the two lines under it. They hold the other five sliders where they are, put this one at 1, and report what your top-ranked dish actually costs (or weighs, or how far it is) on that dimension; then the same at 10. The gap between those two numbers is the entire value of moving that slider, in real units. When both lines match, nothing currently in view changes hands on that dimension, and the slider is doing nothing for you right now.

Underneath: every dish is scored 0–100% on each dimension (best dish currently in view = 100%, worst = 0%), and the six are averaged by these weights. 0 removes that dimension entirely. Only the sliders' sizes relative to each other matter; six sliders at 3 rank identically to six at 9.

Methodology, condensed: prices are real, scraped from restaurant sites, delivery listings, or Google Maps in September 2026; never fabricated. Nutrition (kcal / protein) is a component-based estimate from each dish's description, not lab data, except chain items (Jollibee) and the home-cooked grocery meals, which are marked high confidence. Everything else is medium (detailed description) or low (thin description or weakly-sourced price); treat relative ordering as more reliable than the absolute numbers, and re-verify anything you're about to act on. Grocery meals assume you already own basic cookware and spices; ingredient prices are Loblaws Carlton St shelf prices, not sale prices. Full source notes and per-dish reasoning: repo.
